# Consequence rules v3 (draft, nothing here changes the live site)

Why: Ben's concern on 16 Oct 2026 is that the label must be right on every innings that deserves it, and that tempo (very fast or very slow hundreds) may be another reason a hundred counts. This note sets out the evidence and a clearer rule card to argue over.

## 1. Strike rate: the facts (3,708 hundreds with balls faced, 40 balls or more)

- Average strike rate of a Test hundred: **59.6**. Median 56.8. Middle half 47.7 to 68.1. The fastest tenth are over 80.7, the slowest tenth under 41.1.
- It has crept up with the era: before 1970 **52.0**, 1970-89 54.6, 1990s 55.9, 2000s 62.3, 2010s 61.8, 2020s **66.2**. So "fast" and "slow" must be judged against the decade, not a fixed number.
- Today's tons of consequence are **not** different on tempo: average strike rate 61.0 for tons against 63.0 for good tons. The rule ignores tempo.
- Share that are tons by strike rate: under 40: 27%; 40-50: 21%; 50-60: 24%; 60-70: 23%; 70-85: 19%; 85-100: 19%; 100 and over: 13%. Very fast hundreds are the least likely to be a ton under today's rules.
- Scenario averages: chased it down 69.0, carried 64.1, rescued 62.3, saved the match 52.7 (saves are the slow ones).

## 2. Are deserving innings being missed? (the 150 highest-scored measured hundreds)

87 are tons; 63 are not. The 63:
- 21 draws where the team was never in danger (Tendulkar 241*, Gayle 333, Younis Khan 313, Lara 400*)
- 20 losses (Astle 222, Randall 174, Sangakkara 192)
- 7 dead rubbers (Richards 291, Bradman 299*)
- 11 "other": mostly innings with no match record yet (Hammond, Barrington, Sobers 198), not real rejections
- 3 "came in comfortably" (Brook 317), 1 already safe (Bradman 270)

So the rule is rejecting for stated reasons, and most of the 63 are consistent with the Hello Sport brief (a great ton is not always a ton of consequence). The gaps that are real:
1. **Missing evidence**, not wrong rules: Renshaw was low confidence only because the script had no facts. Fixed by hand, and the same applies to every post-2006 hundred without a ball-by-ball record.
2. **Tempo**: no rule credits an innings that was unusually quick or slow. Candidates are on the tempo tick page.
3. **Losses** are excluded outright. Ben's ruling stands; the alternative is a separate "great losing ton" tag that does not count as a ToC.

## 3. Candidate tempo rule (to test with the tick page, not yet applied)

- **Fast:** a win, the match alive, and a strike rate in the top tenth for its decade (or the hundred reached in 90 balls or fewer). Reason: it changed the time left to win. 80 measured hundreds fit this and are not tons today.
- **Slow grind:** a win or a draw the team was never in danger of losing, strike rate in the bottom tenth for its decade and 250 or more balls. Reason: it ate time or wore the opposition down. 79 measured hundreds fit this.
- Tempo is a reason, never a requirement: middle-tempo hundreds can still count for any of the other reasons.
- Ben's answers on the 60 tick-page cards decide whether either rule is adopted, and at what threshold.

## 4. Rule card (clear version of today's rules, with tempo as a possible fifth reason)

A hundred is a ton of consequence when it passes three questions:
1. **The team won or saved the match.** Never a loss. A draw counts only if the team was behind, following on, or batting in the last innings.
2. **The match was alive.** Not a dead rubber (the series already decided), and the team not already more than 200 ahead before the innings began.
3. **The hundred changed it**, for one of these reasons: chased it down; saved the match; rescued the innings (three down under 100, or four wickets falling around him while under 200); carried the innings (40% of the runs, coming in under 100; 50% from any entry); *or, if adopted, tempo (fast or slow as above)*.

Evidence ladder shown on every verdict:
- **High, ball-by-ball**: the score and wickets when he came in are known.
- **High, scorecard** (Bradman) or **high, match reports** (hand-checked, such as Renshaw): several reports describe how the innings unfolded.
- **Medium, results only**: the result, innings and series state are known; the score when he came in is not.
- **None**: no verdict yet.
