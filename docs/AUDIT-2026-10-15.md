# Tons of Consequence: data audit, 15 Oct 2026

Where the data stands, how good the ton-of-consequence tagging is, what is missing, and ready-to-paste prompts for new conversations.
State: `main` as of this date. All figures were counted from the repo, not estimated, except where marked "my judgement".

## 1. Coverage

| Era | Hundreds | Match result known | Given a ton-of-consequence verdict |

| 1946-69 | 640 | 9 | 16 |

- 367 of the 1,812 are tons of consequence (20%).
- 2,816 hundreds have no verdict; 2,771 of them are before 2006, and 45 are 2006 on (mostly Afghanistan men's Tests, which Cricsheet withholds).
- 2,849 hundreds have no recorded match result at all. That is the cheapest gap to close (see section 3).

## 2. Quality score for the tagging (my judgement, with the evidence)

| Measure | Evidence | Score |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | 39% of hundreds; 98% since 2006, about 1% from 1946 to 2000 | 4 / 10 |
| Fact quality where measured | 1,739 verdicts use ball-by-ball facts (score and wickets on entry, partnerships, lead, series stakes); 29 Bradman and 44 older ones are hand-ruled from scorecards or Wikipedia without the entry score | 8 / 10 for the 1,739; 5 / 10 for the 73 |
| Rule stability | Of the 311 tons the written test gives (before your individual rulings), 275 (88%) would stop being tons under at least one single, reasonable tightening; only 36 survive them all. The total swings from 311 to 103 across the variants in `docs/drafts/consequence-whatif.md` | 4 / 10 |
| Explanations | Every verdict has a fact-only reason; 175 individual rulings plus 44 older rulings are recorded, none attributed to a person on the site | 7 / 10 |
| Independent testing | Hello Sport asked for third-party testing; none yet | 2 / 10 |

Summary: strong and defensible for 2006 on, thin and partly inferred before that, and the headline number depends heavily on the thresholds. The 36 hundreds that pass every tightening are the safest core to quote.

## 3. What data exists that we do not have

Cheap (public, lawful, can be added soon)
1. **Match results for the 2,849 hundreds with none.** Wikipedia's per-player lists ("List of international cricket centuries by ...") give result, innings of the match, batting position, captain and man of the match for every century of about 150 batters. Ponting and Waugh were checked this way on 15 Oct. One pass would also fill batting position and innings number for pre-2006 hundreds.
2. **Series state going in.** Wikipedia tour pages list every Test result, so series score, decider and dead rubber can be worked out for every Test in history, with no ball-by-ball needed.
3. **Team totals for each innings and the lead on entry.** Same tour pages.
4. **Host country per ground, captain flag, keeper flag, handedness, date of birth, debut Test.** Wikipedia and Cricsheet's player register.
5. **Full names.** 129 hundreds (42 players) are still in initials; more Wikipedia checks would clear most.

Harder
6. **The score and wickets when each pre-2006 batter came in.** Needs scorecards with fall of wickets. ESPNcricinfo has them but its terms forbid automated access, so this needs hand collection from Wikipedia match pages or a licensed source (CricketArchive). This is the one thing that would turn "cannot tell" into a real verdict for about 2,700 hundreds.
7. **Afghanistan men's Tests** (45 hundreds): hand-collect scorecards.
8. **Video and stories.** 30 hundreds have a verified video, 36 have a sourced story, the rest a one-line fact summary.
9. **Hello Sport moments.** 8 of about 90 episodes are indexed.

## 4. Suggested improvements, in order of value

1. Fill results, innings, position and series state for all hundreds from Wikipedia (items 1 to 3). Then re-run the test where the facts suffice, and show "result known, entry unknown" clearly where not.
2. Decide the headline rule. Because 88% of tons flip under a small tightening, pick one set of thresholds, publish it on a "What is a TOC?" page, and show how many tons survive the stricter version ("core tons").
3. Show confidence on every verdict (full facts / scorecard / result only) so readers know how much to trust each.
4. Independent test: give Hello Sport's hosts the written rules and 20 innings to judge blind, then compare.
5. Finish the stories project (4,500 one-line summaries) with the weekly task.
6. Per-batter pages: add a "verdict coverage" line so Sangakkara's 7 of 29 (38 hundreds) reads honestly.
7. Add the weekly audit's output to a public "data status" page.
8. Longer term: a player-first view (Ponting's career, every innings, with verdicts), since people look up players more than innings.
